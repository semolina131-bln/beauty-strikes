Korean skincare is known for gentle textures, thoughtful formulas and excellent sunscreens. It is also known for the "10-step routine" — an idea that became a marketing format more than a skincare necessity. You can borrow what works and leave the rest.

## The principle behind K-beauty routines

The useful idea is **layering lightweight products, each with a clear job**, and treating sun protection as non-negotiable. The less useful idea is that more steps mean better skin. Every additional product adds cost, time and another chance of irritation.

## The short routine

### Morning

1. **Rinse or gentle cleanse.** If your skin isn't oily, lukewarm water is often enough in the morning.
2. **Hydrating toner or essence (optional).** Useful for dry or dehydrated skin. Skip it if your moisturizer already feels like enough.
3. **Serum (optional).** One targeted product — for example niacinamide for oiliness or uneven tone.
4. **Moisturizer.** Gel textures for oily skin, creams for dry skin.
5. **Sunscreen.** Broad-spectrum, applied generously as the last step.

### Evening

1. **Oil cleanser or cleansing balm** — only if you wore sunscreen or makeup. It dissolves both more easily than water-based cleansers.
2. **Gentle water-based cleanser.** Low-pH, non-stripping. Skin should feel clean, not tight.
3. **Serum or treatment.** This is where stronger actives such as retinoids usually go.
4. **Moisturizer.** Can be slightly richer than in the morning.

That's it: four to five steps, several of them optional.

## What "double cleansing" is actually for

Double cleansing means an oil-based cleanser followed by a water-based one. It is genuinely helpful for removing water-resistant sunscreen and long-wear makeup. If you wear neither, a single gentle cleanse is enough.

## The steps you can skip

| Step | Skip it if… |
|---|---|
| Essence | Your toner or serum already hydrates well |
| Sheet masks | You're on a budget — they're a treat, not a foundation |
| Eye cream | Your regular moisturizer doesn't irritate the eye area |
| Multiple serums | You can't say what each one is for |

## A note on Korean sunscreens in the US

Many popular Korean sunscreens are formulated and labeled for the Korean market. In the US, sunscreens are regulated as over-the-counter drugs, and the products sold here should carry a US Drug Facts label. Before buying, check the listing and packaging for US-compliant labeling and an SPF and broad-spectrum claim.

## Introducing new products safely

- **One at a time.** Wait one to two weeks before adding the next product, so you know what caused any reaction.
- **Patch test** on the side of the neck or jaw for a few days.
- **Watch the actives.** Avoid starting several exfoliating acids, retinoids or high-percentage serums at once.

## The bottom line

The best Korean-inspired routine is the shortest one that keeps your skin comfortable, clean, hydrated and protected from the sun. Start with cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen. Add a single serum only when you know what you want it to do.
